## Context

The short-form player (`charlla-player`) runs on Next.js SSR hosted on EKS. At a certain point, pods repeatedly returned 504 errors or crashed due to OOMKilled. Although my primary role was frontend engineering, a crashing player directly affected client-facing pages, so I took the initiative to investigate the root causes.

## Problem Definition

While the symptom was singular (an outage), there were multiple candidate causes, none of which fully explained the issue on its own. Definitively blaming a single factor would have left us vulnerable to hitting the exact same wall during the next incident.

## Approach

Instead of hunting for a single culprit, I laid out a chronological timeline of events and evaluated how well each potential factor accounted for the observed symptoms.

## Root Causes

Ultimately, I determined that four overlapping factors caused the failure:

- **HPA Configuration Issue** — Failed to scale properly in response to traffic spikes.
- **Liveness Probe Sensitivity** — Exacerbated the situation by killing pods that experienced temporary slowness.
- **CloudFront Cache Hit Ratio Drop** — Increased the volume of requests hitting the origin server.
- **March 10 Widget Deployment** — The trigger that altered load patterns under the aforementioned conditions.

## Results

I authored a comprehensive incident report outlining each cause along with supporting evidence and shared it with the team. I structured the report so that actionable fixes could be applied at the configuration level.

## Lessons Learned

Production outages rarely stem from a single cause. To prevent recurrence, it is crucial to separate "what triggered the issue" from "why that trigger escalated into a full outage." Furthermore, even as a frontend engineer, ignoring the infrastructure layer makes it impossible to fully explain issues that surface on the user UI.
